Sea level extremes and compounding marine heatwaves in coastal Indonesia

Weiqing Han, Lei Zhang, Gerald A Meehl, Shoichiro Kido, Tomoki Tozuka, Yuanlong Li, Michael J McPhaden, Aixue Hu, Anny Cazenave, Nan Rosenbloom, Gary Strand, B Jason West, and Wen Xing

Journal Nature Communications
Volume 13, Article number 6410
Publication Date October 27, 2022
Article Type Research Article

Abstract

Coastal regions in Indonesia experience chronic flooding due to episodic sea level extremes and subsidence. Here we show that extreme sea levels in three coastal regions are driven by a combination of interannual-to-decadal internal climate variability and long-term sea level rise. Marine heatwaves compound this problem in coastal areas already experiencing more frequent and intense sea level extremes, creating conditions that are detrimental to marine ecosystems and coastal communities. These conditions are exacerbated when sea level extremes and marine heatwaves occur simultaneously. The results identify specific windows of high-impact compound flooding risk driven by the coincidence of internal climate variability, which can inform local operational forecasting and early warning systems. We also find that continued emissions of greenhouse gases will drive substantial increases in future mean sea level and more frequent occurrence of extreme sea levels, with their effects on marine heatwaves potentially magnified by accompanying changes in ocean circulation patterns.

Introduction

Coastal regions worldwide face increasing challenges from sea level rise and extreme weather events. Indonesia, with its extensive coastline and growing population in vulnerable coastal areas, is particularly susceptible to these hazards. The combination of episodic sea level extremes, long-term mean sea level rise, and marine heatwaves creates a complex hazard scenario for coastal communities.

Subsidence in many Indonesian coastal areas further exacerbates the impacts of sea level rise, effectively increasing relative sea level rise and flood risk. Understanding the drivers of extreme sea level events and their interaction with marine heatwaves is critical for developing effective adaptation and mitigation strategies.

Methods

This study employs a combination of observational data, climate model simulations, and statistical analyses to examine sea level extremes and marine heatwaves in three coastal regions of Indonesia. Sea level data from satellite altimetry and tide gauge measurements are analyzed to identify extreme events and their characteristics. Ocean temperature data from multiple sources are used to identify marine heatwave events.

Climate model simulations from the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project (CMIP5) are analyzed to understand potential future changes in sea level and marine heatwave frequency and intensity under different greenhouse gas emission scenarios.

Results

Sea level extremes in coastal Indonesia are driven by a combination of several factors. Internal climate variability, particularly related to the Indian Ocean Dipole and El Niño-Southern Oscillation, creates interannual to decadal variations in sea level. Superimposed on this variability is a long-term sea level rise trend driven by thermal expansion of seawater and ice sheet melting, with regional variations related to changes in ocean circulation patterns.

Marine heatwaves are increasingly common in coastal Indonesian waters and show pronounced seasonal and interannual variability. Critically, we find that marine heatwaves and sea level extremes often occur during similar periods, creating compound events that amplify impacts on marine ecosystems and coastal communities.

Discussion

The results indicate that coastal Indonesia experiences a complex hazard environment where sea level extremes and marine heatwaves interact to create particularly severe conditions. These compound events occur when internal climate variability amplifies both sea level and ocean temperature, creating windows of enhanced risk.

The identification of these high-risk windows has important implications for operational forecasting and early warning systems. By understanding when sea level extremes and marine heatwaves are more likely to coincide, local authorities can better prepare for and respond to these events. This is particularly important for coastal communities already stressed by chronic subsidence and long-term sea level rise.

Looking forward, climate model projections suggest that future greenhouse gas emissions will drive substantial increases in mean sea level across the region. More importantly, the frequency and intensity of extreme sea level events are projected to increase significantly over the coming decades. These changes will be accompanied by shifts in marine heatwave characteristics, with potentially important implications for marine ecosystems and human communities that depend on them.

The mechanisms driving these changes involve both the direct effects of warming on sea level and ocean temperature, as well as indirect effects through changes in ocean circulation patterns. Some regions may experience particularly pronounced changes in marine heatwave intensity due to circulation changes that concentrate heat in coastal areas.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that coastal Indonesia faces a converging hazard environment where sea level extremes and marine heatwaves combine to create compound impacts on marine ecosystems and coastal communities. The identification of specific windows of high-impact compound events driven by internal climate variability can inform local operational forecasting and early warning systems. Continued greenhouse gas emissions will substantially increase the baseline hazard level through mean sea level rise and more frequent extreme events, while potentially also increasing the frequency of compound events through circulation changes that amplify marine heatwaves.
